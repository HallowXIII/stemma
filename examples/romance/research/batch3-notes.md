# Batch 3: adjectives and function words. Disagreements and judgement calls

## Sources actually consulted (full text)
The sources are the same as the pilot (see `pilot-notes.md`), all as archive.org djvu text:
- **NyropI / NyropII**: `grammairehistor01nyro`, `grammairehistori02nyrouoft`. NyropII §261–271 covers adjective declension, §285 the affricate z, and §478–481 superlatives and numerals.
- **Bourciez**: `prcishistoriqu00bouruoft`. Used for §13 (analogical feminines), §20 (proclitic weakening), §49 rem. III (je/jo), §71 and §77 (o before a nasal), §137 (kw), §173 (mauvais) and §188 (l-vocalisation).
- **PidalManual**: `manualelementald00menuoft` (1904). Used for §13, §37–39, §44–47, §63, §89 (numerals), §93–94 (personal pronouns), §100 (article) and §101 (relative).
- **PidalCid**: grammar `cantardemocidte00pidagoog`, used for §49 (apocope), §61–62 (article), §67 (mucho/muy), §68 (numerals), §73 (pronouns) and pp. 192–193 (non/no). **New in this batch** is the vocabulary volume `CantarDeMioCidMenend2`, cited as "PidalCid vocab. s.v. …". The text volume is `CantarDeMioCidMenend3`.
- Wiktionary (en, fr). I read the raw wikitext of the Old French, Old Spanish and Spanish sections.

Cid forms are cited from the text volume. That volume prints both the paleographic and the critical text, together with notes, so raw word counts are about double the real ones. I checked each form in context and give no counts.

I could not reach Pope, Penny, Lloyd, DCECH, CORDE or TLFi, the same gap as in the pilot.

## Disagreements between sources
1. **OF bon vs buen (bonus).** Bourciez §71 says ŏ closed early before a nasal in most of Gaul. *buona* (Eulalie, in the north-east) and *buen* (Norman texts) are regional. fr.wiktionary says proclitic *bon* replaced tonic *buen*. en.wiktionary lists *buen/boen* as alternative forms with no localisation. **Francien c.1150 = bon.** Wiktionary writes the vowel /u/ (/bun/, /ˈbu.nə/). I wrote /o/ to match the pilot's -ons /ons/.
2. **OF je vs jo.** Bourciez (§49 rem. III) says proclitic *je* "apparaît dès le XIIe siècle". Nyrop I says *jo* (like *ço*, *lo*) is found only in the oldest texts. fr.wiktionary has *eo* in 842, *jo* in the 11th c., "et puis *je*". I chose *je* /dʒə/ (medium). The Roland and Alexis have *jo*, and the tonic forms *gié* and *jou* coexist.
3. **l-vocalisation before a consonant (haut, mout, maus, mauvais).** Bourciez §188 says it was accomplished across northern France "vers 1100", even though 12th-c. MSS still write *halt, molt, colp*. en.wiktionary gives OF *halt* /halt/, and fr.wiktionary gives archaic *moult* as [moɫt]. I followed Bourciez and wrote /haut/, /mout/, /maus/, /mauvajs/. Use /halt/ etc. if the model dates vocalisation after 1150.
4. **OF kw in quatre.** Bourciez §137 says the labial "ne paraît pas s'être effacé complètement, devant a du moins, avant la fin du XIIe siècle". Wiktionary gives /ˈk(w)atɾə/, and Nyrop lists the spellings *katre, catre*. I wrote /kwatrə/ (medium). *qui* is /ki/ in every source.
5. **Sp claro: inherited or semi-learned.** Following the brief, it is tagged `semi-learned`. en.wiktionary, citing DCECH, says conserved kl- is uncommon and *\*llaro* would be expected. Pidal (Manual §39.2, 1904) only lists *clarum, claro* among the words where the cluster "vacila" and is kept, next to *clavija, clamor, gloria*. He does not call it learned. The Cid already has *claro*, *clara* and *Montes Claros*.
6. **OSp él vs elle.** PidalCid §73.1 says *el* (vv. 23, 124, 305 …) is the usual form in the Cid MS, with *elle* 3× and *ele* 2×. The scribe replaced the older *elle*. PidalManual §93.3, from 1904, instead calls *elle* the form "usada en el Poema del Cid". I wrote /él/ (the later and more detailed source). The poet's own form may have been *elle*.
7. **OF deus vs dous.** Nyrop §481.2 gives *dous, deus* < duos and nominative *dui, doi* < \*dui. He adds that *dous* in Roland v. 1440 is an Anglo-Norman scribe's form. Wiktionary's headword is *deus* /deu̯s/. Under CONVENTIONS (ō/ŭ > ou) I wrote /dous/. The ou vs eu stage at 1150 is the same open question as in the pilot.
8. **OF cui.** en.wiktionary treats *cui* only as an alternative spelling of *qui*. Etymologically it is the Latin dative *cui*, used as the oblique (en.wiktionary *li* mentions VL dative *cui*). I found no source for its pronunciation, so /kui/ is medium.

## Judgement calls
- **ei > oi (moi, toi, trois).** I followed the pilot's *avoir* /avojr/ choice and wrote /moj/, /toj/, /trojs/. Wiktionary labels *mei/tei* early or Anglo-Norman and *treis* "12th c. or Anglo-Norman". The older values /mej, tej, trejs/ are given in the notes column. *plein* is unaffected.
- **OF ai (mais, malvais, fraile).** Kept as /aj/ as in the pilot. Nyrop I §200 would already have ɛ.
- **OF article les.** I wrote /les/, not /ləs/, because modern /le/ continues a full vowel. I found no source on the vowel's value in 1150.
- **OF longe (F of lonc).** The spelling is secure (fr.wiktionary *lonc*: fs *longe*, fp *longes*). The value of g is not. I wrote /londʒə/ (low), the regular outcome of ng + a, as in *verge* < *virga*. That makes Fr *longue* /lɔ̃g/, which takes its /g/ from the masculine, `analogical`. That classification is mine. I found no source that states it.
- **Analogical feminines in French.** *grande, forte, verte* (and *longue*) are `analogical`. Bourciez §13 says Middle French gave *forte, grande* an -e by analogy with *bonne*. Nyrop II §261 (group 4, *forz < fortis*) and Wiktionary show OF F.NOM.SG = F.ACC.SG = *fort, grant, vert* with no -e. That means F.NOM.SG needed no separate row.
- **OF M.NOM.SG -z = [ts].** *granz, forz, verz, halz, molz* are written /ts/. Nyrop II §268 and §285 say it was reduced to [s] in the 13th c.
- **OF paradigm cells.** Several OF cells (*nueves, haltes, males, cleres, frailes*, all of *molt* except the adverb) are paradigm forms from Nyrop II §261–267 or Wiktionary's declension tables. I did not check them in a text. Their notes say so.
- **altus h-.** Fr *haut* (h aspiré) and OF *halt* have h- from Frankish *\*hauh/\*hōh*, crossed with *altus* (Nyrop I §480.1: "haltus < altum + hôh"; en.wiktionary *halt*). A plain sound-change model will produce *\*aut/\*o*. Spanish *alto* has no h-.
- **malus.** I gave Fr *mal* as an adjective (medium, fixed phrases only). *mauvais* (< \*malifātius, Bourciez §173; fr.wiktionary says the etymology is disputed) is `replaced`. I also gave one OF *malvais* row, since it already competed with *mal* in OF.
- **multus.** OF *molt/mout* is nearly always an adverb c.1150. The adjectival paradigm (*molz, moltes*) is rarer (low confidence). fr.wiktionary says it was variable until the 13th c. Modern *moult* /mu/ is archaic or jocular, and *beaucoup* fills the slot (no row). OSp: the Cid has only *mucho* (plus *muy* and once *much*: PidalCid §67). *muncho* is a later dialectal form (en.wiktionary), not in the Cid, so it gets no row.
- **longus in Spanish.** *luengo/luenga* are inherited, and only the feminine is in the Cid. Modern *luengo* is archaic. *largo* < *largus* has rows with kind `replaced`.
- **OSp grande/grand/grant.** I gave the full form /gɾánde/. PidalCid §49.1 shows that apocope was free (also after the noun and predicatively), and *grand/grant* are the commoner spellings. Model *grand* as a free variant if needed.
- **OSp non vs no.** *non* is the form in the Cid (PidalCid pp. 192–193: 130 *non* against 4 *no* outside enclitic contexts). Loss of -n began before apocopated enclitics (*nol, nos*).
- **OSp verde, seco, luengo (M).** None is in the Cid. They are cited from PidalManual only (medium). OSp initial v- is /β/ (Wiktionary /ˈβeɾde/), following the b/v spelling contrast. The pilot did not settle initial v-.
- **OSp yo.** I wrote /ʝó/, matching modern Spanish. /jó/ is equally defensible for 1200.
- **OSp nueuo.** Only M.ACC.SG and M.ACC.PL are adjectival in the Cid. *nueuas* occurs as the noun 'news'. There is no *nueua*, so it gets no row.
- **Relatives qui/quien.** I wrote them stressed (/kí/, /kjén/) so that ĕ > je in *quem* > *quien* has a stressed vowel. Relatives are usually atonic in the phrase. The modern subject *quien* is `analogical`: accusative *quem* filled the nominative slot when *qui* fell out of use in the 14th c. (PidalManual §101.1). French *qui* in the ACC cell is `analogical` because *cui* merged with *qui*.
- **Tonic pronoun etyma.** OSp/Sp *mí, ti* come from *mihi, tibi* (PidalManual §93.1, PidalCid §73.1). OF/Fr *moi, toi* come from stressed *mē, tē* (Wiktionary). The latin column differs accordingly.
- **ille etyma.** Sp *el* and *él* come from nominative *ille*. OF *le* comes from *illum* (VL \*illu). OF *li* and *il* come from VL \*illī (ille reshaped on *quī*). The plurals come from *illōs/illās*. OSp *el* is also used before feminine a- and e- (*el agua, el espada*: PidalCid §61).
- **duo feminine.** Not requested. OSp has *dues* once in the Cid (v. 255), otherwise *dos* for both genders. OF has *does/doues* only in the East.
