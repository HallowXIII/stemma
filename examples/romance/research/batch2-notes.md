# Batch 2: third-declension nouns and *presbyter* — disagreements and judgement calls

## Sources actually consulted (full text)
- **NyropI / NyropII**, **Bourciez**, **PidalManual**, **PidalCid**: the same archive.org full texts as the pilot (`grammairehistor01nyro`, `grammairehistori02nyrouoft`, `prcishistoriqu00bouruoft`, `manualelementald00menuoft`, `cantardemocidte00pidagoog`). "PidalCid vocab." is the vocabulary volume (`CantarDeMioCidMenend2`), and "PidalCid gram." is the grammar volume. Verse numbers are those Pidal cites.
- **Cid text**: Pidal's paleographic edition (`CantarDeMioCidMenend3`, the part before the *edición crítica*). The counts in the TSV are rough, because the OCR renders ç as g and long s as f, and the page apparatus is counted with the poem.
- **Roland O**: the Oxford MS of the *Chanson de Roland* (c.1100 text, Anglo-Norman copy), from Wikisource's transcription of Mortier's edition: https://fr.wikisource.org/wiki/La_Chanson_de_Roland/Raoul_Mortier. Word counts come from that text. Its spelling is Anglo-Norman (u for o, ei not oi, final -t and -d written), so I used it for the **case forms** and not for Francien phonology.
- Wiktionary (en, fr, es), using the raw wikitext of each page.
- Not used: Darmesteter (nothing there that the others did not cover). Still unreachable: Pope, Einhorn, Zink, TLFi, DCECH and CORDE, as in the pilot.

## How the columns were filled
- **latin**: the Latin form that the cell's form continues: nominative for NOM.SG, accusative for ACC.SG and ACC.PL, and the VL nominative plural in *-ī* (NyropII §239.2: *patres > patri*) for NOM.PL. For imparisyllabics this shows the stem difference directly (*comes / comitem*, *latrō / latrōnem*, *senior / seniōrem*, *presbyter / presbyterum*). Where VL remade the nominative on the oblique stem (NyropII §239.1), it is given as *\*montis*, *\*pedis*, *\*dentis*, *\*pontis*. For neuters (*cor*, *nōmen*, *mare*) the citation form is used throughout, because Latin *corda*, *nōmina* and *maria* are not continued.
- **kind = analogical** is used only for an Old French nominative singular whose *-s* the grammars describe as added: *cuers*, *nons*, and the feminine *nuiz*, *flors*, *citez*. A plural formed in Romance for a neuter (*mers*, *cuers*) is left as `inherited`, with a note.
- **Apocope in the Cid** (*noch*, *mont*, *puent*, *dient*): the IPA gives the full form, and the apocopated spelling goes in `spelling` and `note`. The apocope was a 12th–13th-century trait that was later undone. If the model is meant to produce it, use /nótʃ/, /mónt/, /pwént/.
- No row is given where a form was not found: OSp *madres*, *mares*, *soles*, *leyes*, *flores*, *cibdades*, *puentes* and *prestes*. They certainly existed, but none of the sources I could reach attests them.

## Disagreements between sources
1. **Feminine nominative -s (nuiz, flors, citez).** NyropII §249.2 says that consonant-final feminines had a cas sujet in -s "au temps de Chrestien" (*flors, genz, vertez*), that earlier Norman texts lack it, and that he cannot tell whether it is analogical or a VL remade nominative. en.wiktionary gives *flors* as the nom. sg. of *flor*, but gives *nuiz* only as a plural. Roland O has *la nuit est aserie*, with no s. c.1150 falls between Roland (c.1100) and Chrétien (c.1170–90), so these three rows are **low**. Drop the -s (NOM.SG = ACC.SG) if the model follows the older state.
2. **lei / loi nom. sg.** Wiktionary's declension gives nom. sg. *lei* and plural *lez*. Since *lei* ends in a vowel, Nyrop's -s rule for consonant-final feminines does not clearly apply. I gave NOM.SG = *loi*. Etymologically, *lēx* would give *leis*, so an -s form would also be regular.
3. **soleil, cas sujet.** NyropII §267 gives *soleuz* (ʎ + s > -uz). Roland O writes *soleilz* (and *soleill*, which Gautier corrects to *soleilz*). fr.wiktionary gives *solaus*, which I can't account for. I used Nyrop's form, at low confidence. NyropI §185 calls *soleil* "pas entièrement populaire": the regular pretonic outcome would be *souleil*, which is attested.
4. **prestre / provoire.** Nyrop (§260) declines them as one word: cas sujet *prestre*, cas régime *proveire*. en.wiktionary calls this "unlikely", on the grounds that the two rarely occur together, and treats them as separate words with *prestre* the commoner. Both agree on the etyma (*presbyter* nom. and *presbyterum* acc.). NyropII §273 shows *prestre* already used as a régime in OF. Modern *prêtre* continues the cas sujet (NyropII §281), and *provoire* survives only in the Paris street name Rue des Prouvaires.
5. **sire.** Nyrop says *senior > sendre* (Oaths of Strasbourg) or *sire*; Wiktionary writes *sendra*, which is the spelling in the Oaths. Wiktionary and fr.wiktionary derive *sire* from a contracted or denasalised *\*seior*. fr.wiktionary also calls *sire* the cas sujet of *sieur*, while Nyrop derives *sieur* from *seniorem* alongside *seigneur*.
6. **Old French dent: gender and vowel.** Wiktionary has it masculine in OF and feminine from Middle French. Its IPA gives both /dent/ and /dant/. Bourciez §61 dates en > an in the Île-de-France to about 1050, so I wrote /dant/.
7. **OF vowel of free ō (flor, seignor).** I followed CONVENTIONS (ou). Bourciez §72 says the MSS of the 11th–12th c. write o, that the sound was probably already a diphthong, and that eu came in the second half of the 12th century, in the North-East first. Wiktionary writes /u/ (*flor* /fluɾ/) or /oː/ (*seignor*).
8. **cuer.** Wiktionary gives /kwɛɾ/ with an alternative [kwœɾ]; Roland O spells it *coer* or *quer*. I followed CONVENTIONS (ue as wɛ), which also gives *cuens* /kwɛns/ where Wiktionary has /kuɛns/.
9. **OSp preste.** Pidal (Manual) says it came "quizá … por intermedio del francés", and Wiktionary says plainly that it was borrowed from OF *prestre*. The conventions have no `kind` for a loan from a sister language, so I used `replaced`. It should not be derived from Latin by the Spanish rules.
10. **corazón etymon.** Wiktionary gives *\*corāceōnem*; other handbooks reconstruct *\*corātiōnem*. I used Wiktionary's form.

## Answers to the brief's specific questions
- **frater in Old Spanish.** The Cid has only *hermanos* (written with h, < *germānōs*). *fradre* is attested in Berceo (*S. Millán* 44, 85, c.1230–50), against the Occitan-type *fraire* in *S. Domingo* (PidalCid gram. p.141), mostly for monks. So it is later than 1200 and narrower in meaning; the row is **low**. *flaire* is Asturian or Catalan, not Castilian, and Spanish *fraile* is an Occitan-type loan.
- **sol vs soleil in OF c.1150.** Only *soleil* is found, already in Roland O (c.1100). No OF *sol* 'sun' was found: en.wiktionary's OF *sol* has only the senses from *sōlus* and *solidus*. So the diminutive had replaced the simplex before the first texts, and the French rows are `derived` with latin *\*sōliculum*. Old Spanish keeps *sol* (Cid, masc.).
- **cor in Spanish.** Old Spanish has both. *cuer* < *cor* appears in the Cid (4x), but only in set phrases (*de cuer*, *por cuer*). The ordinary noun is *coraçón* (Cid, many times), given as `derived`. Modern Spanish keeps the reflex of *cor* only in *de cor*.
- **canis in Spanish c.1200.** The Cid has *canes* (2263, an insult) and no *perro*. Wiktionary dates *perro* to c.1200 as a pejorative synonym, which later displaced *can*. I gave *perro* in Old Spanish as `replaced` at low confidence. Modern *can* is literary.
- **cité: inherited or learned?** It is inherited. Bourciez §141 and NyropI §444 treat *civ(i)tate > cité* as regular popular development (v lost before a consonant, pretonic i syncopated). fr.wiktionary dates OF *citet* to c.950. The doublet *cit* < Merovingian *civitem* is given as a `derived` row (NyropII §251 Rem.).
- **sire and seigneur in modern French.** *sire* is the OF cas sujet, which became a separate word (a title of address), and *seigneur* is the cas régime. NyropII §281 says the accusative forms *seigneur* and *sieur* survived beside *sire* "with a differentiation of use".
- **comes.** OF *cuens* (cas sujet, < *cómes*, with the diphthong from stressed ŏ; *cons* when proclitic) against *conte/comte* (< *com(i)tem*, with ŏ checked after syncope). French *comte* has a relatinised spelling. Old Spanish *conde* is usual in the Cid (41x), with *cuende* 3x.
- **latro.** OF *lerre* (< *látro*, tr > rr, free a > e) against *larron* (< *latrṓnem*). Modern *larron* continues the oblique. Old Spanish has *ladrones* in the Cid (349).

## Judgement calls for c.1150 OF / c.1200 OSp
- **ei > oi.** As in the pilot (*avoir*), I wrote oi for Francien c.1150: *roi, rois, loi, provoire*. The Roland spellings *rei*, *reis*, *lei* and *proveires* represent the older value /ej/.
- **Masculines in -e (pere, frere, lerre, prestre, sire).** There is no -s in the cas sujet (NyropII §254). The analogical *peres* and *freres* appear in Anglo-Norman early but are general in Francien only by the end of the 13th century.
- **Final dental after a vowel** is gone c.1150 (*cité*, *pié*), as for 3sg -et in the pilot. Roland's *citet* and *pied* are spellings only. Before -s it survives as z = /ts/ (*piez*, *citez*, *nuiz*).
- **nuit.** Bourciez §69 says ui was still a falling diphthong at the end of the 11th century and became rising later. I wrote /nyjt/. *noit*, the early and western form, is what Roland O mostly writes.
- **s before t (prestre).** Bourciez §157 says it was still pronounced at the end of the 11th century and gone everywhere on the continent about a century later. I kept /s/ for 1150.
- **Nasal vowels** are not marked, following CONVENTIONS. Bourciez §77 confirms that the nasal consonant was still articulated after a nasalised o (*larron*), so /larron/ is a reasonable broad form.
- **OSp cibdad**: /b/, because Pidal takes the spelling *çipdad* (v.397) as evidence of a stop.
- **OSp y between vowels**: written j for Old Spanish (*reyes* /réjes/) and ʝ for modern Spanish (/réʝes/). This follows the rule in CONVENTIONS that Spanish ʎ is kept apart from ʝ. If you want one symbol across the stages, normalise it.
- **OSp hermano**: /ermáno/. The h is purely graphic (from *g-*), so the convention for h- from Latin f- does not apply.
